Common Emergency Water Heater Problems

Unexpected water heater problems can happen with gas, electric, or tankless systems. Identifying the issue early may help reduce additional inconvenience and determine the appropriate repair.

No Hot Water

A complete loss of hot water is one of the most common emergency situations. The cause may involve electrical components, gas system issues, heating elements, burners, thermostats, or internal equipment failures.

Water Heater Leaking

Leaks may develop around valves, plumbing connections, fittings, or the storage tank. Prompt inspection helps identify the source of the leak.

Burst Water Heater

A burst water heater can release significant amounts of water into the surrounding area. Immediate inspection is recommended to determine the extent of the damage and discuss available solutions.

Water Heater Overflow

Overflowing water may indicate excessive pressure, valve problems, or another system malfunction requiring professional evaluation.

Water Heater Not Heating

A water heater that produces little or no hot water may require diagnostics to identify issues with heating elements, burners, thermostats, ignition systems, or other components.

Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit

Gas water heaters rely on a properly functioning pilot light. If the flame repeatedly goes out, the system should be inspected before continued use.

Electrical Problems

Electric water heaters may experience heating element failure, thermostat issues, wiring problems, or tripped breakers that prevent normal operation.

Tankless Water Heater Errors

Tankless systems may display diagnostic error codes related to ignition, water flow, sensors, or heat exchangers. An inspection can help identify the underlying cause.

Rusty Hot Water

Discolored water may indicate corrosion or deterioration inside the tank or plumbing system.

Water Heater Making Noise

Popping, rumbling, or banging sounds may result from sediment buildup or other mechanical issues affecting performance.
