Common Gas Water Heater Problems

Gas water heaters contain several components that work together to provide dependable hot water. Over time, these parts can wear out or develop issues that affect system performance.

Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit

A pilot light that repeatedly goes out may indicate a worn thermocouple, gas supply issue, or another component requiring inspection.

No Hot Water

A complete loss of hot water may result from ignition failure, burner problems, gas supply issues, or a malfunctioning gas control valve.

Ignition Failure

Modern gas water heaters may use electronic ignition systems. If ignition fails, the burner may not light, preventing the water from heating.

Thermocouple Failure

The thermocouple is a safety device that monitors the pilot flame. If it fails, the gas supply may shut off, preventing normal operation.

Gas Burner Problems

A dirty or damaged burner can reduce heating efficiency and affect overall performance. A professional inspection can determine whether cleaning or replacement is needed.

Gas Control Valve Issues

The gas control valve regulates gas flow and water temperature. If it malfunctions, your water heater may not operate correctly.

Water Heater Leaking

Leaks can occur around valves, fittings, plumbing connections, or the tank itself. Identifying the source of the leak helps determine the appropriate repair.

Venting Problems

Proper venting is important for safe operation. Damaged or blocked venting should be inspected by an independent professional.

Sediment Buildup

Mineral deposits naturally accumulate inside storage tanks over time. Excess sediment may reduce efficiency and decrease hot water capacity.

Rusty Hot Water

Discolored water may indicate corrosion inside the tank or deterioration of internal components. An inspection can help determine the cause.
