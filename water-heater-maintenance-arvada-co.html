What Does Water Heater Maintenance Include?

Hot water heater maintenance can involve several checks depending on the type of water heater.

For a traditional tank-style system, a professional may inspect the water tank, thermostat, heating element or burner, anode rod, dip tube, pressure relief valve, and visible connections.

The exact maintenance procedure depends on the unit and its condition.

Water Heater Flushing and Sediment Removal

Sediment can accumulate inside a tank over time. This buildup may affect the way the system operates and can reduce available tank space.

A professional water heater flush may help remove accumulated sediment from the tank.

Whether flushing is appropriate depends on the water heater's condition, age, and maintenance history.

Anode Rod Inspection

The anode rod helps protect many traditional tank-style water heaters from corrosion. As the rod wears down, the tank may become more vulnerable to corrosion.

A water heater anode rod inspection can help determine its condition and whether professional replacement may be appropriate.

Pressure Relief Valve Check

The pressure relief valve is an important safety component on a storage water heater. A professional may inspect the valve and related components as part of water heater servicing.

Pressure concerns should be evaluated professionally rather than handled through unsafe DIY modifications.

Temperature Adjustment

A water heater should operate at an appropriate temperature for the household or property's needs. A professional may check the thermostat and discuss temperature adjustment when necessary.

Correct settings can help balance hot water needs with practical energy use and scald-risk considerations.

Water Heater Leak Inspection

A water heater leak inspection can help identify where water is coming from and whether the issue involves a fitting, valve, connection, or the tank itself.

Finding a leak early can give property owners more time to understand their options before the problem becomes more disruptive.
